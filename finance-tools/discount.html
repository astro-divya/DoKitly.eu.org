Questions people ask about Discount Calculator

Are two 10% discounts equal to 20% off?

Not when applied sequentially: the second 10% applies to the already-reduced price.

Can a larger discount percentage still produce a higher final price?

Yes. Two items starting at different prices can have different final prices regardless of which shows a larger percentage reduction.

Why is 20% followed by 10% not 30% total?

The second percentage uses the already reduced price, producing 0.8×0.9 = 0.72 of the original.

Can a flat ₹500 coupon beat a 20% discount?

Yes, depending on the original price and coupon restrictions. Compare the actual payable total in each scenario.

Sale price and money actually saved

A 20% discount on a listed price of ₹1,500 reduces the price by ₹300, leaving ₹1,200 before any additional taxes or fees. The amount saved and final payable amount are different outputs, so label both before sending a price quotation. If the original price was inflated just before a promotion, a large displayed percentage does not prove the deal is worthwhile. Compare the discounted payable amount against other current offers and check the same product specification rather than relying entirely on the advertised percentage.

For two successive discounts of 20% and 10%, start from ₹1,000: after the first discount the price is ₹800, and after the second it is ₹720. The combined reduction is 28%, not 30%, because the second 10% applies to a smaller base. By comparison, if a store simply advertises a single 30% discount on ₹1,000, the price is ₹700. These promotions may look similar in an advertisement but they produce different bills. Work out each step on its correct base.

Discount versus tax and shipping

A discount can be applied before or after tax depending on how a seller computes the invoice and applicable rules. For example, 10% off ₹2,000 gives ₹1,800. If a 5% tax applies to that new taxable amount, the next step would add ₹90, giving ₹1,890. If shipping costs ₹150, the amount actually paid rises to ₹2,040. A discount-only calculator cannot infer an unknown tax treatment, delivery fee, coupon exclusion or local invoice rule. Treat its answer as one component in the checkout calculation, not a guaranteed cash-register total.

When a coupon says "save up to ₹500", the cap may reduce the benefit for high-priced items. Twenty percent of ₹4,000 is ₹800, but a ₹500 maximum would leave only ₹500 off if the cap applies. Conversely, a minimum-order condition may invalidate an otherwise correct calculation. Read the promotion conditions and enter the amount eligible for discount, which might exclude certain items. For a cart containing differently discounted products, compute each qualifying item's price separately before combining the results.

Common retail mistakes and checks

To validate the savings, subtract the discounted price from the listed price; divide the saving by the ORIGINAL eligible price to recover the percentage. A ₹250 saving on ₹1,250 is exactly 20%. Do not divide by the reduced ₹1,000 price, which would produce 25% and answer a different question. For wholesale comparisons, look at quantity and unit price after discount. A 10-pack costing ₹900 after promotion is ₹90 per item, while a six-pack costing ₹600 is ₹100 per item, even though the overall order total is lower.

Returning a discounted product

A return may be refunded at the amount actually paid rather than the original ticket price. If an item lists at ₹1,000, is discounted by 20%, and is purchased for ₹800 before applicable fees, treating the refund as ₹1,000 creates a bookkeeping discrepancy. In exchanges, a new promotional price can differ from the earlier purchase price. Save the receipt and distinguish discount savings from refund value; the calculator cannot infer a retailer's refund policy. This distinction matters when reconciling cash records after seasonal promotions.

Stacked discounts and payable amount comparisons

A jacket priced at ₹2,000 with 20% off costs ₹1,600 before any applicable taxes or delivery fees. A second 10% discount applied to that reduced price yields ₹1,440, so the combined reduction is 28%, not 30%. Always identify whether successive offers apply before or after tax and whether minimum basket conditions exist. When comparing stores, calculate the final payable amount rather than just ranking the displayed percentages. If the interface also shows savings, verify that savings equal the original reference price minus the resulting price. Coupon caps can make an advertised percentage misleading for a large order.
