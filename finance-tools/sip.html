Questions people ask about SIP Calculator

Does an assumed 12% return mean investments will earn 12%?

No. It is an input for modelling, not a guaranteed or predicted outcome.

Why might two SIP calculators give different totals?

They may assume deposits at the start versus end of a period or use different compounding conventions.

Does a projected SIP value include inflation automatically?

Not unless the tool explicitly calculates real purchasing power. Inflation can reduce what the future amount buys.

What if I miss a monthly contribution?

The actual total invested and compounding periods change. Recalculate using the contribution schedule you really followed.

Regular contributions and compounding are separate choices

A systematic investment plan usually means putting money into an investment on a repeated schedule rather than depositing one lump sum on day one. Suppose ₹2,000 is contributed monthly for 12 months. Total contributions are ₹24,000, regardless of the assumed return. Future value depends on the assumed monthly growth rate and whether contributions occur at the beginning or end of each period. A calculator can estimate future value from a chosen return assumption; it cannot guarantee that market prices will follow a smooth constant growth path.

At an illustrative monthly growth rate of 1%, end-of-month contributions of ₹2,000 for 12 months accumulate to roughly ₹25,365, whereas beginning-of-month contributions accumulate slightly more because each instalment gets one extra month to grow. The choice of contribution timing should match the calculator's convention. When annual returns are quoted, check whether monthly rate is simply annual nominal rate divided by 12 or converted from an effective annual rate; those approaches are not identical.

Interpreting invested amount and estimated gains

Future value and invested principal are different. If 36 instalments of ₹3,000 were made, the invested amount is ₹1,08,000. If a scenario estimates ₹1,25,000 at the horizon, its projected gain is ₹17,000 before charges and taxes. That gain is hypothetical, not interest promised by a savings account. Market investments can experience negative returns, volatile monthly prices and periods when the current value is below total contributions. The calculator is a planning aid, not a return prediction engine.

An inflation check helps interpret distant amounts. A corpus of ₹10 lakh in ten years may buy fewer goods than ₹10 lakh does today. If spending rises at 5% annually, a current expense of ₹1 lakh would become roughly ₹1.63 lakh after ten years. This does not change the nominal future-value formula but provides context for long-term targets. For an investment goal, compare estimated corpus with inflation-adjusted future expense and consider risk instead of focusing on one optimistic number.

Plausibility checks and limits

A zero-return SIP should end at total contributions, assuming no fees, because money has not grown. Increasing either the number of instalments or contribution amount should increase the nominal total under ordinary nonnegative-return assumptions. These sanity checks catch years entered as months, rupees entered in thousands, or a percent interpreted as a decimal fraction. Actual mutual fund units, NAV changes, exit loads, expense ratios and taxes require separate records; they are not produced by an idealized steady-return SIP calculation.

Lump sum versus monthly schedule

Investing ₹1,20,000 once at the beginning of a year differs from contributing ₹10,000 at the end of each of twelve months, even though invested principal totals ₹1,20,000. The lump sum is exposed to investment returns for longer, while each monthly contribution enters at a different time. With falling markets, that timing may produce a different relative outcome than in rising markets. A SIP estimator should not be interpreted as a lump-sum calculator without explicitly changing the financial model.

Investment contributions versus projected market returns

A monthly contribution of ₹2,000 over ten years is ₹2,40,000 of principal before any investment growth. A projection assuming a positive annual return estimates a future value, but actual market returns fluctuate and can be negative. Contributions made at the beginning versus end of each period accumulate for different lengths of time, so calculators may disagree even when the headline assumed return matches. Review the assumptions before interpreting a chart as a guaranteed maturity amount. Fund expense ratios, taxes, inflation and withdrawals affect the spendable amount and cannot be inferred from a single forecast percentage.
