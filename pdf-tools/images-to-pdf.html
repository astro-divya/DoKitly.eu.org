Common questions about Images to PDF

Will a photo-to-PDF conversion make the text selectable?

No, not by itself. OCR or a real text layer is required.

Why is the resulting PDF not searchable?

Putting an image inside a PDF container does not automatically recognize the words visible in that image.

Will combining JPG photos produce a Word-like editable document?

No. The PDF generally stores images of the pages rather than editable text paragraphs.

How can I keep an image from being stretched on the page?

Preserve its aspect ratio when fitting it within the selected paper dimensions, allowing margins if necessary.

Make an ordered PDF from images

Choose photos in the correct reading sequence

Photographed notes often arrive out of order. Check the first and last page before assembling the file, and rotate sideways photos so that text reads naturally.

Create and check the pages

Add your images, arrange their sequence and create the PDF. Open the result and scan each page to confirm that no sheet is missing, upside down or unexpectedly cropped.

Practical example: photographed assignment

If you photographed six handwritten pages, arrange the images by page number rather than relying on filenames or upload order. Review diagrams and signatures in the final PDF.

Understand the limits

An image-based PDF preserves photographs of words; it does not automatically turn handwriting into selectable text. Use a separate OCR workflow if editable text is required.

Quality and privacy checks

Use clear photos with good contrast, keep your originals, and verify that small handwriting is legible at normal zoom before sharing a document.
