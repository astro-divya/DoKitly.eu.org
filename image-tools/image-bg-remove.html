Common questions about Background Remover

What should I inspect besides the transparent preview?

Place the result against both light and dark backgrounds to reveal halos and missing edges.

Why can a removed background leave a coloured halo?

Edge pixels may contain a blend of foreground and original background colours; segmentation alone does not always remove that fringe.

Why does a fine object sometimes disappear with the background?

Automatic segmentation may classify narrow or low-contrast details as background when confidence is low.

Will saving the result as JPEG preserve transparency?

No. JPEG has no alpha channel; use a supported transparent format such as PNG when you need a cutout.

Choose an image with a clear subject

Clear contrast between the subject and background makes isolation easier. Similar colors, shadows, loose hair and transparent glass are harder for automatic processing.

Run the available background removal

Upload an image and run the removal process. Inspect the resulting subject against both light and dark backgrounds before accepting the result.

Practical example: product listing

For a mug on a busy table, check the mug handle opening, rim and contact shadow in the processed preview. These areas can reveal leftover background or removed subject pixels.

Transparency and export formats

Use a format that supports transparency, such as PNG, when you need a transparent output. JPEG does not retain an alpha channel, so a transparent result can become a solid background.

Limitations worth checking

Fine hair, reflections and transparent objects can produce imperfect edges. Review at full size and keep the original photograph rather than assuming every image will produce a flawless cutout.
