How to remove a watermark from an image

  1. Choose an image and open Remove Watermark.
  2. Use Auto Detect. If it misses the watermark, use Select Area and draw one rectangle around it.
  3. Review the automatic or selected area in the preview.
  4. Use the Before / After slider to compare the original and cleaned result.
  5. Select Remove Watermark to rebuild the result at full resolution, inspect it, then download.

Watermark removal is reconstruction, not recovery of hidden original pixels. Simple backgrounds usually reconstruct more naturally than detailed faces, text, hair or complex textures. Always inspect the final image.

Add a watermark to an image

Use Add Watermark to enter your text, choose color and opacity, rotate it and select a preset position. You can also drag directly on the processed preview for custom placement. This is useful for branding, drafts, proofs and ownership labels.

Checking visibility without claiming automatic ownership

A watermark adds visible text or an image mark to a picture, often for attribution or discouraging casual reuse. A photographer might place a small studio credit over a corner of a preview, while a stock-image proof could use a repeated semi-transparent overlay across the middle. These have different trade-offs. A corner credit preserves more of the photograph but is easy to crop away; a central mark is harder to avoid but can distract viewers. No visible watermark guarantees copyright protection or prevents determined removal.

Opacity, position and scale should be checked against the whole photograph. Ten-percent opacity may look subtle on a dark background yet disappear over a light sky. An oversized mark can cover important product details or facial features. For batch branding, test several photos with varied brightness before applying the same settings to all. A watermark that looks balanced on a square picture may be badly positioned on an ultra-wide banner if coordinates are fixed in absolute pixels rather than proportional to dimensions.

Attribution, rights and honest claims

Putting your name on another person's picture does not make it yours. Only watermark material you own or are authorized to modify, and retain original licensing information as needed. Conversely, removing or covering rights information can have legal consequences. A watermark is a visual communication element; it is not a substitute for copyright registration, usage terms or file provenance metadata. For client deliveries, consider whether the watermark belongs on previews only while purchased originals remain clean.

When the watermark is text, check font support for the intended language and its readability after compression. If it uses a logo image, verify that the logo's transparency and color match the source background. In a JPEG export, transparent overlay edges will be flattened into ordinary pixels. Avoid repeatedly adding a watermark to the same already-watermarked image, because the marks will stack and the result will be difficult to reverse.

Checking the exported picture

Compare the original and final file to confirm that only the intended overlay was added and that the image dimensions are preserved unless a separate resize was requested. Inspect several points to see whether the opacity varies over contrasting regions. Reopen the download rather than judging only a browser preview. If a watermark appears pixelated, use a higher-resolution logo or suitable vector source when supported. Store an unwatermarked original in a safe location so a new attribution style can be applied later without removing old pixels.

Position consistency in a collection

When watermarking images in different orientations, a position like "bottom-right" should maintain a sensible margin from each corner. A fixed 80-pixel offset means something different on a 200-pixel thumbnail than on a 4000-pixel photograph. Check several aspect ratios and consider proportional sizing if supported. If a platform later crops the image, the watermark may be cut away; placing it farther inward can help at the cost of covering more original content.

Understanding the limits of watermark reconstruction

When removing an overlay from a photograph, the pixels under lettering may not exist in the exported source image. An inpainting or clone-based result estimates what could have been there; it cannot establish the actual hidden detail. Compare the repaired area at 100% zoom for repeated textures, mismatched edges and unnatural colour gradients. Transparent watermarks across faces and text are especially difficult because the original information is obscured. Use the editor only for images you own or are authorised to alter, and retain a copy of the original. If the watermark is part of a document verification mark, deleting it may misrepresent the source.

Common questions about Add & Remove Watermark from Images

Can watermark removal restore the exact hidden pixels?

Not reliably. Pixels under an obstruction are not present to recover exactly.

When should I keep the original instead of an edited result?

If removal changes essential subject details, creates obvious artifacts or the underlying image rights do not permit alteration, use a properly licensed clean source.

What helps when removal leaves visible smears?

Work with the largest authorized source image available, refine the affected selection, and inspect repeated textures and edges at full size. Inpainting predicts missing detail; it cannot guarantee restoration of the original hidden pixels.

How can I check a watermark-removal result?

Compare the affected area with surrounding textures and edges at full resolution, and keep the authorised original for reference.
