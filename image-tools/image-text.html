Positioning legible text over an image

Adding text to a photograph is useful for social announcements, banners and annotated instructions. When the output is a PNG or JPEG, the letters are usually rendered into the image pixels and are not a separately editable text layer. Keep a copy of the original and record the exact wording so it can be updated later. A screenshot saying "Sale ends Friday" will become outdated after the promotion; unlike HTML text, pixel text cannot be changed by modifying a web page's caption alone.

Choose font size relative to the actual output dimensions. A 24-pixel label may look readable on a 600-pixel-wide banner but tiny on a 3000-pixel-wide export when both are shown at the same physical size. Verify horizontal alignment and safe margins, especially if the image will be cropped into a square or portrait preview. A long sentence can wrap or clip unexpectedly, so use the real intended wording rather than a short sample while testing placement.

Contrast and accessible meaning

White text over a bright sky can disappear even when the font is bold. Place captions over a suitable dark overlay, shadow or solid backing when the tool and design permit it; check the result on both desktop and phone. Decorative lettering does not replace accessible HTML or social-platform alt text for screen-reader users. If the graphic contains essential event details such as date, address or emergency instructions, also provide them as ordinary text in the publication.

A small watermark-like name in a corner serves a different purpose from a title across the center. For a photography credit, avoid covering the subject's face; for a promotional heading, allow enough room for visual hierarchy. Consider placement for right-to-left languages and long Hindi words, which can require appropriate font support and shaping. A font that lacks a required glyph may show boxes or missing characters; inspect the actual rendered download rather than trusting the preview alone.

Export and quality checks

For an instructional screenshot, PNG often preserves crisp text edges better than a heavily compressed JPEG. For a large photo with minimal lettering, a suitable JPEG quality may reduce size effectively. Reopen the file at full resolution and zoom into the letters for jagged edges, clipping or unintended background artifacts. If the tool offers a reset, use it before preparing a different design rather than stacking text from earlier attempts. Verify exact spelling, phone numbers and times before publishing because mistakes in pixel text are harder to correct downstream.

Check exact wording at final scale

An image with a promotional offer may be rendered beautifully but still contain a wrong currency symbol or expiry date. Before exporting, confirm those characters in the editor, download the file and inspect at the scale recipients will actually see. If the text is legally material, such as conditions for a promotion, putting only tiny wording on an image may be insufficient for accessibility and consumer clarity. Pair the graphic with an accessible text description in the publication.

Text-overlay readability at the final banner size

Adding “SALE TODAY” to a 1200-pixel-wide banner requires a different font size than adding it to a 300-pixel thumbnail. Check the downloaded result at its intended display size, not only in a large editing workspace. If you place white text over a bright area, a shadow or contrast panel may improve legibility, but heavy effects can distract from the subject. An exported PNG or JPEG generally bakes the text into the pixels; it is not equivalent to retaining an editable layered document. Verify punctuation, spelling and placement before downloading multiple copies for different social networks.

Common questions about Add Text to Image

Why does a caption look readable in the editor but not on mobile?

The displayed image may shrink; test typography at the final viewing size.

Are overlays editable inside an exported PNG?

Not as separate text objects in the ordinary raster image; keep any editable source design separately.

Can I edit the overlaid text after exporting a flat PNG?

Not as an independent text layer. Return to the tool with the original image or keep a separately editable source.

Why might emoji glyphs render differently after download?

Font availability and browser text rendering can change glyph shapes or fallback behaviour.
