Questions people ask about Statistics Calculator

Which statistic resists extreme values better?

The median is usually less sensitive to a single outlier than the arithmetic mean.

What does standard deviation not tell me?

It measures spread under a chosen convention, but by itself does not establish normality, causation or whether data were collected fairly.

Why can variance change when I switch sample and population modes?

The denominators differ: sample variance typically uses n−1 as an estimate of population variability.

Does correlation near zero prove the variables are unrelated?

No. There can be nonlinear relationships or subgroup patterns not captured by a single linear coefficient.

Summary statistics reveal different aspects of data

For the values 2, 4, 6 and 8, the mean is 5 while the median is also 5. Adding an outlier 100 raises the mean to 24 but changes the median only to 6. This is why the mean can misrepresent a typical observation in a skewed dataset. Choose the summary that answers the question rather than assuming every average means the same thing. The mode is the most frequent value, and a dataset can have no unique mode or several modes depending on its frequencies.

Variance and standard deviation describe spread. For the values 2, 4, 6, 8, the population mean is 5 and the squared deviations sum to 20, giving population variance 5 and standard deviation √5≈2.236. If these four observations are a sample used to estimate a larger population, sample variance instead divides by 3, giving 20/3≈6.667. Confusing these two conventions explains many discrepancies between calculators, spreadsheets and textbooks. Check which one the selected interface actually reports.

Correlation is not a causal conclusion

A strong correlation between variables X and Y describes how they vary together under a chosen statistic; it does not establish that changing X causes Y to change. For example, ice-cream sales and heat-related incidents may both rise on hot days because temperature affects both. Before interpreting a correlation coefficient, inspect a scatterplot for outliers, nonlinearity and sample size. A coefficient near zero can conceal a curved relationship, and a value near one can be driven by a few extreme points in a small sample.

If the interface accepts separate X and Y series, enter the same number of paired observations in both and preserve row order. Rearranging Y values independently changes the correlations even though the individual sets of numbers remain the same. For a simple check, X=[1,2,3] and Y=[2,4,6] have perfect positive linear correlation, whereas pairing Y=[6,4,2] reverses the trend. Verify the tool's treatment of missing values before calculating from incomplete records.

Reading numerical results responsibly

A descriptive-statistics tool computes summaries from the numbers supplied; it does not assess whether the sample is representative, whether measurements are independent or whether values were recorded correctly. A sample of only three observations can be summarized accurately but is rarely sufficient for strong general conclusions. Document the dataset size, units and any exclusions along with the reported mean, variance or correlation. For research reports, check whether rounding, missing entries and outliers were handled consistently across all software involved.

Comparing two classroom score distributions

Consider two classes whose five test results are A = [60, 70, 70, 80, 90] and B = [70, 70, 70, 70, 90]. Their averages are 74 and 74, yet the spreads differ: class A ranges from 60 to 90, while B clusters at 70 with one higher mark. That makes the mean alone an incomplete description. If using the optional paired-data field for correlation, both series need the same number of observations in corresponding order; mixing independently sorted X and Y values destroys the original pairs. Compare mean, median, range and standard deviation together, then investigate unusual observations rather than deleting them automatically.

Outliers, population variance and mean versus median

Consider results 2, 3, 3, 4 and 88. The mean is 20, while the median is 3; the unusually large final value pulls the mean away from the typical observation. This is a reason to inspect the distribution, not to claim that one summary statistic is always best. If the interface reports variance, identify whether the formula divides by n or by n−1; population and sample variance differ even on the same values. Correlation describes association, not proof that one variable causes another. When comparing groups, consider sample size, outliers and missing values instead of reporting only a precise-looking decimal.
